## Chimney repairs in Leeds
A chimney is the most exposed bit of brickwork on any Leeds roof. It takes wind-driven rain from every side, freezes and thaws all winter, and most of the stacks across the city are well over a hundred years old. When something gives, it is usually one of four things: the lead flashing where the stack meets the roof, the mortar joints, the flaunching that holds the pots, or the pots themselves.

## The chimney repairs we're asked for most in Leeds

### Chimney flashing repair
Leaking flashing is the most common reason for a damp patch on a chimney breast or a stain on the bedroom ceiling next to it. Old lead dries out, splits and lifts, and joints that were "sealed" with mastic years ago open up again. A proper repair renews the lead (stepped flashing on the sides, apron at the front, back gutter behind) and re-wedges it into the mortar joints, rather than smearing sealant over the gap.

### Repointing and pointing
Crumbling or missing mortar lets water into the stack and eventually into the loft. On Leeds' Victorian and Edwardian brick and gritstone stacks, the mortar should be matched to the age of the building: a hard modern cement on soft old brick traps moisture and makes the bricks spall. For warning signs and what repointing really costs, see our [chimney repointing cost guide](/blog/chimney-repointing-cost-signs-england/).

### Flaunching and chimney pot replacement
The flaunching is the sloped mortar cap that holds the pots in place. Once it cracks, water runs straight into the stack and pots work loose in high wind. Re-flaunching is often combined with re-bedding or replacing cracked pots, and with fitting cowls or caps on flues that are no longer used.

### Chimney rebuild and restoration
A leaning stack, badly spalled brickwork or a chimney that has been patched too many times may need taking down to sound brickwork and rebuilding. On older properties around Headingley, Roundhay and Chapel Allerton, installers can match reclaimed brick or stone so the stack still looks right.

### Capping or removing a disused chimney
Plenty of Leeds homes have stacks that haven't been used for decades. A disused chimney still needs sound pointing, flaunching and flashing. Otherwise the choice is to cap it (with ventilation, to avoid damp in the flue) or take it down below roof level and make good the roof.

## Frequently asked questions
**How do I know if my chimney flashing is leaking?**
Damp or staining on the chimney breast or on the ceiling right next to it, especially after wind-driven rain, is the classic sign. From outside, look for lead that has lifted, split or been covered in sealant.

**Who pays to repair a chimney shared with my neighbour?**
A chimney stack that straddles two properties is usually shared, so the cost of repairs is normally split between the owners. Check your deeds and agree the work with your neighbour before it starts.

**Do I need scaffolding for a chimney repair?**
Not always. Small jobs like re-bedding a pot or patching flashing can sometimes be done from a roof ladder, but repointing or rebuilding a stack safely usually needs scaffolding or a tower, which is a big part of the price.

**Can I remove a chimney stack on a house in a conservation area?**
Removing or visibly altering a chimney in a conservation area, or on a listed building, can need permission from Leeds City Council. Check with the planning team before committing to the work.
