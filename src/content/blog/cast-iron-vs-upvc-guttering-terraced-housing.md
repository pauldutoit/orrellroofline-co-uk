Most guttering comparisons online treat this as a simple upgrade decision. Across the North West's huge stock of Victorian and Edwardian terraces, it's more often a repair-vs-replace question, and the two materials behave very differently once you're past that.

## Lifespan, realistically

Cast iron, properly maintained - painted every few years, joints resealed as they age - can last well over a century. Left unmaintained, it corrodes from the inside and can fail in a fraction of that time. uPVC needs virtually no maintenance and typically carries a 25-30 year guarantee, but it doesn't have cast iron's multi-generational lifespan when properly cared for.

## Where each one wins

- **Cast iron wins** on listed terraces and conservation areas, where matching the original material and profile is often required, and on rows where the ironwork is decorative as well as functional.
- **uPVC wins** on cost, on maintenance, and on rear elevations or non-sensitive properties where matching the original material doesn't matter.

## The maintenance reality most people underestimate

Cast iron that's never been painted is living on borrowed time - rust doesn't announce itself until a joint fails or a section perforates. If you've bought an older terraced property and don't know the guttering's maintenance history, a professional inspection before deciding to repair or replace tells you far more than guessing from the pavement.

## Cost over a 30-year horizon

uPVC is cheaper upfront and needs no repainting. Cast iron costs more to repair initially, but if the existing ironwork is sound, repairing rather than replacing is usually still cheaper than a full uPVC swap - and on a listed terrace, uPVC may not be an option at all (see our [guide to listed building consent](/blog/listed-building-consent-guttering-fascia-england/)).

## A practical decision path

1. Is the property listed or in a conservation area? If yes, get a planning steer before assuming uPVC is even an option.
2. Is the existing cast iron structurally sound, just cosmetically tired? Repair and repaint is usually the better value.
3. Is the iron corroded through in multiple places? Full replacement - matching cast iron on sensitive elevations, uPVC elsewhere - usually makes more sense than patching a failing system.
