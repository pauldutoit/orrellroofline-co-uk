Greater Manchester and much of Lancashire sit well above the England average for annual rainfall, and that has a direct, practical effect: fascia and soffit problems that generic UK guides describe as taking 15-25 years to appear can show up meaningfully sooner on an exposed North West property, particularly on older timber boards that were never properly maintained.

## Damage to the boards

Check for flaking or cracking paint, and any rotting or crumbling wood if you have timber fascias or soffits. If timber feels soft to the touch, crumbles, or shows dark staining, rot is likely present and replacement is usually the right call rather than another repaint.

## Structural problems

Look for fascias pulling away from the wall, or guttering that isn't being properly supported any more. If gutters are pulling away, sagging or leaking at joints, the fascia behind them is very often already failing too - the two problems tend to arrive together.

## Water damage

Marks on soffits or on the wall below the roofline usually mean water is getting past a damaged board. On a property with persistent gutter overflow (see our [gutter cleaning guide](/blog/gutter-cleaning-frequency-north-west-england-by-region/)), this is often the fascia's first visible sign of trouble, well before the gutter itself is replaced.

## Ventilation and pest issues

Damp or pests in the loft space can point to soffits that aren't providing proper protection any more. Holes in fascias compromise their ability to support roof tiles and guttering; holes in soffits make it easier for dirt, pests and water to get into the loft.

## Age-related wear, adjusted for local rainfall

The usual guidance is 15-25 years for timber and 25-30 years for uPVC. On an exposed North West property with heavier-than-average rainfall, particularly anywhere with limited sun exposure or overhanging trees, it's worth having timber roofline checked somewhat earlier than that range would suggest - waiting for the full 25 years on a north-facing, tree-shaded property is a common way to end up dealing with rot that's already spread into the roof timbers.
