Roofing and roofline work has an outsized reputation for cowboy operators, mostly because the work itself is hard for a homeowner to inspect closely, and a bad job can look fine from ground level for months before it fails. Knowing what a fair quoting process looks like is the best protection.

## What a fair quote process looks like

- An in-person or photo-based assessment before a firm price, not a number given over the phone with no inspection at all
- A written, itemised quote breaking down materials, labour and access separately
- No pressure to sign or pay a deposit on the spot
- A clear timeline and start date, not a vague "we'll fit you in"
- Willingness to answer questions about materials, guarantee terms, and their own insurance without hesitation

## Classic red flags

- **Unsolicited doorstep offers**, especially "I noticed a problem with your roof while working nearby" - a well-known cold-call tactic, not a genuine coincidence
- **Pressure to decide immediately**, often with a "today only" discount that doesn't stand up to scrutiny
- **Cash-only, no invoice** - makes it much harder to pursue a complaint or claim on insurance later if something goes wrong
- **A quote dramatically below others** with no clear explanation - sometimes it's a genuinely competitive rate, but it's worth asking specifically what's different before accepting
- **Reluctance to provide insurance details or a written guarantee**
- **Requesting a large deposit before any work starts** - a modest deposit is normal, a majority-upfront payment is a risk if the company disappears

## What to do if you suspect a problem after the fact

If work has already been done and you're concerned about quality, get a second, independent opinion from a different installer before raising a dispute - a written assessment of what's wrong is far more useful than a general sense that something feels off, both for pursuing a complaint and for getting it fixed properly.

## Comparing quotes properly

Getting 2-3 quotes for anything beyond a minor repair is the single most effective protection against both being overcharged and ending up with a poor-quality installer - it gives you a genuine baseline to judge any individual quote against.
