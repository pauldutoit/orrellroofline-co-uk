"Clean your gutters twice a year" is reasonable generic advice, but the North West's own geography creates real differences in how hard that advice needs to be followed - from the wet, Pennine-facing towns to the comparatively drier Cheshire plain and eastern Yorkshire.

## Why the Pennines change the picture

Weather systems moving in off the Atlantic and the Irish Sea drop a lot of their rain as they meet rising ground - which is why towns on the western, Pennine-facing side of Greater Manchester and Lancashire see notably more rainfall than areas further east, on the other side of the hills.

## A region-by-region starting point

- **Greater Manchester & the Pennine towns (Manchester, Bolton, Oldham, Rochdale, Bury):** among the wetter parts of the region. Two to three cleans a year, more on tree-lined streets or terraces with shared downpipes.
- **Lancashire coast (Blackpool, Preston, Lancaster):** high rainfall plus direct exposure to Irish Sea weather. Similar to the Pennine towns - two to three cleans a year, and pay particular attention after autumn storms.
- **Cumbria border (Carlisle, Barrow-in-Furness):** genuinely among the wettest parts of England, close to the Lake District. Three cleans a year is a reasonable baseline here, not an over-caution.
- **Cheshire plain (Chester, Crewe, Warrington):** flatter, more sheltered ground with comparatively lower rainfall than the Pennine towns. Twice a year is usually sufficient.
- **West Yorkshire & South Yorkshire (Leeds, Bradford, Sheffield):** sitting on the drier, eastern side of the Pennines. Twice a year covers most properties, adjusted upward near mature trees.
- **Stoke-on-Trent & the Midlands border:** typically drier still. Twice a year, or once for a well-exposed, tree-free property.

## Rainfall isn't the only variable

Overhanging trees matter more than regional rainfall for most individual properties - a well-treed street in a drier region can need more frequent clearing than an exposed, tree-free property in a wetter one. Treat the region as a baseline and adjust up for mature trees, a flat or low-pitch roof, or a history of overflow.

## Best times to schedule it

Autumn, after leaf-fall but before winter storms, is the single most useful clean of the year across the whole region. A second clean in early spring clears winter debris and grit before it compacts. If you're only managing one clean a year anywhere in the North West, make it the autumn one.

## What happens if you skip it

Overflow doesn't stay a guttering problem for long - water running down the wall instead of the downpipe leads to damp brickwork and rotten fascias (see our [guide to fascia and soffit warning signs](/blog/signs-fascia-soffit-need-replacing-north-west-rainfall/)). A blocked gutter in a hard freeze can also split as trapped water expands, turning a cleaning job into a repair job.
