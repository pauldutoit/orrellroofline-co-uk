The North West's combination of damp, mild temperatures and long summer daylight hours is close to ideal growing conditions for moss - which is why roofs and guttering here often show significant growth within a few years of a clean, faster than in drier parts of the UK.

## Why it's worse here specifically

Moss needs consistent moisture and shade to establish, and the North West provides both more reliably than much of eastern England - frequent light rain rather than occasional heavy downpours keeps surfaces damp for longer, and tree cover common around Greater Manchester and Lancashire housing provides the shade moss favours. North-facing roof slopes, which get less direct sun to dry out, are almost always the worst affected.

## Why moss is more than cosmetic

- **It holds moisture against the roof surface**, which accelerates decay of the material underneath - whether that's slate, tile, or the felt underlay beneath them.
- **It sheds into gutters**, where it's one of the most common causes of blockage, often faster than leaf litter alone.
- **On older brick and stone properties**, moss and algae growth around a gutter run is often the first visible sign of an overflow problem that's been running for a while.

## Removing it safely

Pressure washing directly on slate or aged tile can do more damage than the moss itself, loosening tiles or stripping protective granules. A soft-brush clean followed by a proprietary moss treatment is generally safer for the roof covering, and treating rather than just scraping reduces how fast it comes back.

## Preventing regrowth

Copper or zinc strips fitted near the ridge release trace metal ions when it rains that inhibit moss growth on the slope below - a low-maintenance preventive measure worth considering on a roof that's replaced or re-covered, particularly on shaded, north-facing sections.
