Named storms reliably bring a spike in fascia, soffit and guttering damage - slipped tiles taking a fascia board with them, gutters torn off by wind loading, downpipes ripped away. What catches most homeowners out isn't whether their policy covers storm damage at all - almost all standard buildings policies do - it's what an insurer actually accepts as storm damage once someone's had a proper look.

## What "storm cover" actually means

Standard UK buildings insurance covers storm damage as standard, not usually as a paid add-on. The cover responds to damage caused by wind, driving rain or hail during a genuine storm event. What it doesn't cover is damage that was already present and simply got worse, or was revealed, by the storm.

## The wear-and-tear problem

This is where most roofline claims run into trouble. If a fascia board was already rotten, or guttering brackets were already loose from years without maintenance, and the storm was what finally brought them down, an insurer's assessor can reasonably argue the storm didn't cause the damage - it just exposed pre-existing deterioration that isn't covered. This isn't insurers being difficult for the sake of it; policy wording genuinely draws that line, and it's the single most common reason a roofline claim gets reduced or refused.

## What to do in the first 24 hours

- **Photograph everything before touching anything** - the damage itself, and ideally the surrounding area, to show the scale of what the storm did generally.
- **Make safe, temporary repairs only.** You're entitled to stop further damage (a tarpaulin over an exposed section, for instance) without needing insurer sign-off first, but keep receipts.
- **Clear blocked guttering if it's safe to do so** - standing water sitting in a damaged gutter often makes things worse before an assessor arrives.
- **Report it promptly.** A claim made weeks after a storm, once the obvious storm-damage pattern has faded, is more likely to face a wear-and-tear challenge than one reported immediately.

## Getting a written report from your installer

A written assessment from whoever inspects the damage - ideally before any repair work starts - is worth far more to a claim than photos alone. Ask specifically for the report to note whether the failure looks storm-caused (a clean break, a section torn from otherwise sound fixings) versus long-term deterioration. That distinction is exactly what an insurer's assessor will be looking for.

## The excess, and when it's worth claiming at all

Storm-damage excess typically runs from around £100 to £500 depending on your policy. For a relatively small repair - a single fascia section or a short run of guttering - it's worth checking the likely repair cost against your excess before opening a claim at all, since a claim, even a successful one, can affect your renewal premium.

## Preventing the next one

The properties that come through a storm cleanly are almost always the ones with roofline that was already sound - secure brackets, no existing rot, gutters that weren't already overflowing. A pre-winter check is genuinely the cheapest insurance you can buy against a much larger claim later.
