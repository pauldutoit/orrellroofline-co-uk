Solar panel installers focus on the panels, the mounting and the electrics - understandably, since that's the job they're being paid for. What often gets less attention is what fitting panels does to the roofline underneath and around them, which can cause problems that only show up well after installation.

## Why roofline condition matters before panels go up

Solar panels typically stay in place for 20-25 years, and once fitted, they make the roof underneath significantly harder and more expensive to access for repairs. A fascia, soffit or guttering issue that would have been a simple job before installation can mean partially dismantling panel mounting to fix afterwards. Checking roofline condition before panels go up, not after, is genuinely the right order.

## Guttering and rainwater flow changes

Panels can alter how water sheds off a roof - runoff can concentrate at panel edges rather than spreading evenly across the roof surface, occasionally overloading a specific section of gutter that wasn't previously taking that volume. This is worth asking your solar installer about specifically, since it's not always considered as part of a standard panel installation, and it interacts directly with whether your existing guttering is correctly sized in the first place (see our [drainage sizing guide](/blog/downpipe-drainage-sizing-building-regulations-england/)).

## Roof penetrations and flashing

Mounting brackets are fixed through the roof covering, which means new penetration points that need proper flashing to stay watertight - done well, this is no different from any other roof penetration; done poorly, it's a new potential leak source that wasn't there before.

## A sensible sequence for panel installation

1. Get roofline and roof covering checked and any necessary repairs done before panels are fitted, not after
2. Ask the solar installer specifically how mounting penetrations will be flashed and sealed
3. Check whether panel placement concentrates runoff onto any particular gutter section, and whether that section needs upgrading
4. Factor future roofline access into the plan - know in advance what's involved if a repair is ever needed under the panels

## If panels are already fitted

An existing installation with roofline issues underneath still needs addressing - it's just a more complex, usually more expensive job involving coordination between a roofline specialist and whoever installed the panels, rather than a reason to ignore the problem.
