Soffit vents look like a minor detail, but they do a specific structural job: letting air move through the loft space so that moisture generated inside the house doesn't condense on cold roof timbers. Get this wrong and you can end up replacing roof timbers that a fascia and soffit job could have protected.

## Where the moisture actually comes from

Everyday activities - showers, cooking, drying laundry indoors, even breathing overnight - put a surprising amount of water vapour into the air inside a house. Warm, moist air rises and finds its way into the loft space through gaps around loft hatches, light fittings and pipework. Without proper ventilation, that vapour has nowhere to go and condenses on the coldest surfaces it finds, which in a loft is usually the underside of the roof and the roof timbers themselves.

## What happens if the loft doesn't breathe

- Condensation forms directly on roof timbers and the underside of the roofing felt or membrane
- Persistent damp encourages timber rot, particularly in older properties with less breathable roofing felt than modern membranes
- Insulation that gets damp performs far worse and can encourage mould growth
- In cold weather, condensation can freeze and then drip as it thaws, mimicking a roof leak that isn't actually there

## How soffit ventilation solves it

Vented soffits let outside air flow in at the eaves and, ideally, out again at a ridge vent or similar outlet higher up the roof, creating continuous airflow through the loft space that carries moisture out before it can condense. This is why soffit replacement is a genuine opportunity to fix a ventilation problem, not just a cosmetic upgrade - solid, unvented soffit boards fitted years ago are a common reason older properties develop loft condensation issues.

## Signs your loft isn't ventilating properly

- Visible moisture or dark staining on the underside of roofing felt when you look up into the loft
- A musty smell in the loft space
- Mould on stored items or on the roof timbers themselves
- Frost forming on the underside of the roof in winter

If any of these sound familiar, it's worth having ventilation checked alongside any roofline work - adding proper vented soffits during a fascia and soffit replacement is a small extra step at that point, but a much bigger one to retrofit later.
