Cladding gets far less coverage than fascia, soffit and guttering, but it's a common add-on quote across the North West - usually to cover exposed gable ends, dormer cheeks, or to modernise a tired elevation without a full render job.

## Typical costs by material

| Material | Typical fitted cost | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|
| uPVC shiplap | £50-£70 per m² | Gable ends, dormers, budget-conscious jobs |
| Composite (wood-effect) | £70-£90 per m² | Visible elevations where appearance matters |
| Timber | £60-£90 per m² plus ongoing treatment | Conservation areas, period properties |
| Fibre cement board | £80-£110 per m² | Coastal or highly exposed sites |

## Why weather changes the calculation

Timber cladding needs re-treating every few years to stop it greying and rotting - a real ongoing cost that's easy to leave out of a like-for-like comparison with uPVC. In exposed or coastal locations along the Lancashire coast, fibre cement or good-quality composite tends to outlast both uPVC and timber, which is why it costs more upfront (see our [coastal roofline guide](/blog/coastal-blackpool-southport-roofline-salt-air-storms/)).

## Where cladding is used on a typical roofline job

- Gable ends on a dormer bungalow, where the apex is otherwise bare render or exposed timber
- Dormer window cheeks, common on loft conversions across Greater Manchester
- Covering an old, failing render finish without the cost of full re-rendering
- Matching a fascia/soffit replacement job aesthetically across the whole roofline

## Planning considerations

Cladding a street-facing elevation on a listed building or inside a conservation area is treated the same way as fascia and guttering changes - it can need consent, particularly if it changes the building's appearance materially (see our [listed building consent guide](/blog/listed-building-consent-guttering-fascia-england/)). Check with your local council before committing to a material choice if your property falls into either category.
