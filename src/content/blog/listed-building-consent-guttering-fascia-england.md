Historic England's own advice is direct on this point: a rainwater system that works and doesn't leak is important to the conservation of a listed building, and clearing gutters of leaves is straightforward maintenance. Consent only becomes an issue once you're replacing or materially altering the rainwater goods - which is exactly the situation most roofline quotes fall into.

## The basic rule

Replacing a cast-iron gutter with cast iron, like-for-like, generally won't need listed building consent. Replacing it with a different material - uPVC being the obvious example - is a genuine change to the building's character, and is far more likely to need consent, even on a run most people would never look at twice.

## Listed building vs conservation area

These are two different things that often get confused:

- **Listed building:** the building itself is on the statutory list. Consent requirements apply to the whole building, inside and out, not just the parts visible from the street.
- **Conservation area:** a defined area where the council has extra control over external appearance, even for buildings that aren't individually listed. Chester's city centre, parts of Lancaster, and older cores of towns across Cheshire and Lancashire commonly fall into this category.

A property can sit in a conservation area without being listed at all, and still face material and colour restrictions on anything visible from a public road.

## What tends to get refused

- Swapping cast iron for uPVC on a street-facing elevation of a listed building
- White uPVC where the original was painted timber or cast iron
- Loss of decorative ironwork (hopper heads, ornate brackets) during a job billed as "like-for-like" that wasn't actually like-for-like
- A different gutter profile on a principal elevation

## What usually goes through without issue

- Genuine repair in matching material, same profile, same position
- Rear elevations not visible from a public road, where councils are typically more flexible
- Repainting in the same or a previously approved colour

## Before you commission any work

Contact your local council's planning department - not Historic England directly, which advises on policy but doesn't grant consent for a specific property - and ask two things: is the property listed or in a conservation area, and does the planned work need consent. Get the answer by reference number rather than relying on a verbal steer, particularly if a neighbour has previously raised an objection about similar work nearby.
