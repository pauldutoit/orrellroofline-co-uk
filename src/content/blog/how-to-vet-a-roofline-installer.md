Roofline work is one of the easier home-improvement categories for an unqualified or uninsured tradesperson to take on convincingly - the visible finish can look fine from the ground for a while even if the underlying work is poor. A few concrete checks separate a competent installer from a risky one.

## Companies House registration

A registered limited company has a filing history you can actually check - incorporation date, filed accounts, registered address. This doesn't guarantee quality, but a business that's been trading and filing accounts for several years is a materially different risk than one with no verifiable history at all.

## Insurance

Ask specifically for proof of public liability insurance and, ideally, employer's liability if they have staff. A legitimate installer will produce this without hesitation; reluctance or vague answers are a real warning sign.

## Trade body membership

FENSA, Assure/Certass, or membership of a recognised trade association isn't compulsory for roofline work the way it is for some window installations, but it's a useful additional signal - it usually means a level of vetting and a complaints process that a completely unaffiliated trader doesn't have.

## Guarantees, in writing

A written guarantee on both materials and workmanship - not just a verbal assurance - should specify what's covered and for how long. Ten years is a common benchmark for installation work; anything offered only verbally isn't worth much if something does go wrong later.

## References and reviews on an independent platform

Reviews on a platform the installer doesn't control are harder to fake and easier to verify than testimonials on their own website. A pattern of similar, recent, specific reviews mentioning roofline work is more useful than a handful of generic five-star ratings.

## How they quote

A detailed, itemised quote - materials, labour, access/scaffolding, disposal - that's willing to be put in writing is a good sign (see our [red flags guide](/blog/free-roofline-quotes-red-flags-cowboy-roofers/)). Pressure to decide on the spot or a quote dramatically below others without an obvious reason are the classic warning signs.

## A simple pre-work checklist

- Company registered and trading history checkable
- Public liability insurance confirmed, ideally seen not just claimed
- Written guarantee on materials and workmanship
- Independent reviews, not just testimonials on their own site
- Itemised written quote, no pressure to decide immediately
