Blackpool's seafront and Southport's coastal streets sit fully exposed to weather coming straight off the Irish Sea. Salt-laden air is a genuinely different problem for roofline materials than the rain and wind that dominate further inland, and it's worth treating differently.

## Why salt air is harder on roofline materials than rain alone

Salt deposited by sea spray is corrosive to untreated metal fixings and brackets in a way plain rainwater isn't - it accelerates rust on steel screws and brackets, and can dull and pit lower-grade aluminium over time. Properties within a mile or two of the Blackpool or Southport seafront see this happen noticeably faster than equivalent properties a few miles inland.

## What that means for material choice

- **Fixings matter as much as the boards.** Stainless steel screws and brackets resist salt corrosion far better than standard galvanised steel.
- **uPVC itself is largely unaffected** by salt air, which is one reason it performs well on this coastline despite the exposure - the vulnerability is mostly in metal components.
- **Standard aluminium can pit over years of direct coastal exposure;** marine-grade aluminium or a powder-coated finish holds up considerably better.
- **Older cast-iron guttering on Victorian seafront terraces** needs more frequent repainting than the same material would inland, since salt accelerates corrosion once paint starts to fail.

## Wind loading, not just salt

Blackpool's seafront and Southport's coastal streets also see higher sustained wind than sheltered inland towns, which matters for how guttering and fascias are fixed, not just what they're made of. Secure, storm-rated brackets at closer spacing are worth the modest extra cost on a fully exposed elevation - particularly relevant given how often this stretch of coast features in storm damage claims (see our [storm damage insurance guide](/blog/storm-damage-roofline-insurance-uk/)).

## A sensible inspection interval

Properties within sight of the water are worth inspecting annually rather than the two-to-three year interval that's reasonable further inland - catching early corrosion on a bracket or fixing is a small job; catching it after a section has failed in a storm is a much bigger one.
